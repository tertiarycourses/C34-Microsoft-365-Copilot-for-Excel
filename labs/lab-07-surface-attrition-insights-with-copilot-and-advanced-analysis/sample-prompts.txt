Microsoft 365 Copilot for Excel (C34) — Lab 7: Surface Attrition Insights with Copilot and Advanced Analysis
Sample prompts for Copilot in Excel. Copy one at a time, adapt it, and verify every result.

[1] Open question
What's interesting about this data in relation to Left_Company? Give five patterns with numbers and group sizes.

[2] Baseline
What is the overall attrition rate in tblAttrition? Show the numerator and denominator.

[3] Comparison
Show attrition rate and headcount for Engagement_Score below 3 vs 3 and above.

[4] By group
Show attrition rate and number of employees by Department, sorted highest first.

[5] Advanced analysis
Enter advanced analysis mode. Compare leavers and stayers on overtime, engagement, promotion, compa-ratio and training.

[6] Correlation
What is the correlation between Overtime_Hours_Month and Engagement_Score, and what does it mean for interpreting the attrition patterns?

[7] Outliers
Are there any managers (Manager_ID) whose teams have unusually high attrition? Only include teams with at least 10 people.

[8] Challenge
Which of my insights could be a coincidence or caused by another factor? Explain.

[Step 1]
Table   : tblAttrition (400 employees active on 2025-07-01)
Outcome : Left_Company = Yes / No (left within 12 months)
Template: Insight_Log sheet

[Step 2]
CHAT ONLY:
What's interesting about tblAttrition in relation to Left_Company? Give up to five patterns, each with
the numbers and group sizes behind it. Do not change the workbook.

ALLOW EDITING:
Add the patterns you just gave me below the last row of Prompt_Log, one row each, with Decision
set to "Unverified".

[Step 3]
CHAT ONLY:
What is the overall attrition rate in tblAttrition (Left_Company = "Yes" divided by all employees)?

CHECK (Allow editing):
On a sheet named Checks (create it if needed), add these formulas with a label beside each,
and tell me the results:
=COUNTIFS(tblAttrition[Left_Company],"Yes")
=COUNTIFS(tblAttrition[Left_Company],"Yes")/ROWS(tblAttrition[Employee_ID])

[Step 4]
CHAT ONLY:
Using tblAttrition, show attrition rate AND number of employees for:
1) each Department;
2) Engagement_Score below 3 vs 3 and above;
3) Overtime_Hours_Month above 20 vs 20 or below;
4) Years_Since_Promotion 3 or more vs below 3;
5) Compa_Ratio below 0.9 vs 0.9 and above;
6) each Work_Mode.

CHECK (Allow editing):
On the Checks sheet, add this formula for Engagement_Score below 3 and tell me if it matches:
=COUNTIFS(tblAttrition[Engagement_Score],"<3",tblAttrition[Left_Company],"Yes")/COUNTIFS(tblAttrition[Engagement_Score],"<3")

[Step 5]
Enter advanced analysis mode.
Compare employees who left with those who stayed on Overtime_Hours_Month, Engagement_Score,
Years_Since_Promotion, Compa_Ratio and Training_Hours. Show the mean for each group, the group sizes,
and the correlation between Overtime_Hours_Month and Engagement_Score. Explain the method.

FOLLOW-UP: Which rows and columns does your code use, and how does it handle blanks?

CHECK (Allow editing):
On the Checks sheet, add these formulas and tell me if they match your means for leavers:
=AVERAGEIFS(tblAttrition[Overtime_Hours_Month],tblAttrition[Left_Company],"Yes")
=AVERAGEIFS(tblAttrition[Engagement_Score],tblAttrition[Left_Company],"Yes")

[Step 6]
CHAT ONLY:

one limitation (sample size, correlation is not causation, overlapping factors). Keep each under 30 words.

ALLOW EDITING:
Write these four findings into the Insight_Log sheet, one per row, with the numbers, the OBSERVED or
HYPOTHESIS label, the explanation to test and the limitation.
